# C01 · Moving out

[Capstones](README.md) · [Mastery rules](../curriculum/mastery.md) · [Evidence sheet](../templates/assessment-record.md)

**Time:** three sessions of about 20 minutes. These are fictional multi-skill assessments. Use current local guidance for real decisions.

## Context

Iman plans to move in four weeks. Compare the fictional options, protect essentials, and prepare the first week of independent living.

## Materials

| Item | Option A | Option B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rent | 600/month | 700/month |
| Utilities estimate | 140/month | 100/month |
| Transport | 120/month | 40/month |
| Stated upfront costs | 600 rent + 600 deposit + 150 moving | 700 rent + 700 deposit + 100 moving |
| Journey | Last bus 20:15; occasional work ends 21:00 | Step-free route not yet verified |
| Terms | Repairs clause incomplete | Cancellation/notice wording incomplete |

Take-home income: 1,500/month. Other essentials: food 220, phone 25, debt payment 80. Available move fund: 1,550, separate from the recurring budget. New home's emergency/access plan is unknown. Contract and deposit amounts are fictional and do not establish legal requirements.

## Your task

Produce a monthly budget and separate upfront-cost comparison. Check journey/access constraints and list contract questions. Make a private-document inventory using fictional labels, a first-week food/cleaning/laundry plan, and a household emergency-plan outline. Explain a provisional choice and what must be verified before paying or signing.

Session 1: analyse and verify. Session 2: produce calculations, messages, and plan. Session 3: review your reasoning and handle the variation. Record any prompts or help; close the explanation for an independent attempt.

## Advanced variation

Income falls to 1,250 and Option B's route is unavailable for a week. Revise the budget and transport plan before committing.

## Suggested solution

<details>
<summary>Open after completing your attempt</summary>

Recurring housing/transport is 860 for A and 840 for B. With other essentials of 325, totals are 1,185 and 1,165, leaving 315 and 335 before other costs and savings. Upfront costs are 1,350 and 1,500; both fit the stated 1,550 fund, leaving 200 or 50. B's recurring cost is lower but its upfront buffer is much smaller. A's late shift needs another verified transport option; B's access needs checking. At income 1,250, remaining amounts fall to 65 or 85 before other costs, making missing costs and disruption important. A responsible answer can choose either, defer, or seek advice; it must explain affordability, access, written terms, verification, and gaps. Include offline emergency contacts and safe household responsibilities. A written menu does not demonstrate cooking.

There may be several defensible choices. Assess the reasoning, verification, calculations, and feasible actions rather than agreement with a preferred life choice.

</details>
