# Mastery: evidence of what you can do

[Curriculum](../README.md) · [Domain criteria](dependency-map.md) · [Progress tracker](../templates/progress-tracker.md)

## Four stages

| Stage | What it means | Evidence required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foundation | Understands the basic concept | Explains it correctly and produces the domain's small first output |
| Applied | Performs with guidance | Completes the task with a checklist and records the help used |
| Independent | Handles a realistic situation without prompts | Produces a correct, feasible response before opening the solution |
| Advanced scenario | Adapts when circumstances change | Handles the variation, explains tradeoffs, and recognises limits |

Every domain has four specific, observable criteria on its [skills page](dependency-map.md). Assess those criteria, not a general feeling of confidence. Advanced scenario means adaptable everyday judgement; it does not mean professional qualification.

## Reading is not demonstration

Track these separately:

1. **Read:** opened the lesson.
2. **Understood:** explained its central idea and corrected misunderstandings.
3. **Practised:** attempted an exercise, possibly with help.
4. **Demonstrated independently:** met the relevant criterion without prompts.
5. **Retained later:** met it again on a fresh delayed task.

Retained later is a reassessment result, not a fifth mastery stage. A learner may demonstrate an advanced scenario today but still need a later retention check. Previous 0–3 confidence ratings can remain as historical notes; do not convert them automatically into these stages.

## Assess an attempt

Use the [assessment record](../templates/assessment-record.md) and mark each criterion **met**, **assisted**, or **not yet**.

- Check calculations, verification, feasible actions, and explanations.
- Record whether a guide, checklist, partner, AI, or suggested solution supplied help.
- Verify sources openly when the task requires it; using an official source is part of independence. A source that gives factual information differs from a prompt that solves the assessment for you.
- A memorised model answer is practice, not evidence of transfer. Use changed materials for reassessment.
- An unsafe action, exposed secret, unsupported entitlement, or invented medical instruction means the relevant criterion is not yet met, even if other calculations are correct.
- Assess only what the output demonstrates. A meal plan is not evidence of cooking; a first-aid description is not evidence of CPR technique.

Award a stage only when its criterion and the necessary earlier criteria have evidence. Prior experience can supply that evidence through a diagnostic or fresh demonstration; rereading every lesson is unnecessary. Assess each domain separately during capstones.

## Revisit and adapt

Start with reviews around 3, 7, and 21 days after practice. These are practical scheduling suggestions, not universal scientific thresholds. Use [fresh reassessment cards](reassessment.md), and adjust intervals when a skill is forgotten or easily retained. Record **retained**, **needed help**, or **not retained**, then choose the next practice action.

Allow spoken, typed, visual, or supported communication and accessible alternatives. Record the adaptation without treating an access aid as lack of ability. Do not grade feelings or personal life circumstances. Use fictional details and a neutral alternative to distressing scenarios.

For health, emergencies, law, and money, recognising when and how to seek qualified help can be an independent action. Never create a real hazard, change treatment, make a payment, or disclose sensitive documents to prove mastery.

## Subskill evidence

Each domain now has observable subskill criteria with separate knowledge, independent and adaptation routes. Domain stages derive only the relevant legacy criterion. The [adaptive evidence rules](adaptive-learning.md) explain assistance, fresh exposure groups, configurable delayed checks and practical observation gates. Self-reviewed dashboard records are provisional educational evidence.
