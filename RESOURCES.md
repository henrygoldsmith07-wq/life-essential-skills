## How to evaluate a new resource

Prefer official authorities, recognised professional bodies, and original research where appropriate. Check authorship, dates, relevant jurisdiction, evidence, and financial incentives. Separate official rules from personal opinions and distinguish examples from personal recommendations.

Guides on communication, decision-making, planning, and administration include original teaching frameworks. Their hypothetical examples do not claim to be findings from a specific study. Linked references support the stated subject areas; they do not imply that every exercise has been individually clinically or experimentally validated.

## Build your local resource list

In a private local worksheet, identify your emergency authority, healthcare service, consumer regulator, tax authority, employment advice service, housing information, first-aid provider, and community services. Record what each covers and when you last checked it.
