# S07 · A payment email that sounds familiar

[Scenario library](README.md) · [Guide](../guides/07-digital-safety.md) · [Competencies](../domains/digital-safety.md)

**Time:** about 20 minutes; split practical tasks across sessions. All people, documents, amounts, and events are fictional. Use local official guidance for real situations.

## Context

Taylor expects an invoice from a fictional supplier. An urgent message arrives. All addresses below are deliberately non-routable examples; do not visit them.

## Materials

```text
From: accounts@harbour-invoices.example
Subject: Final reminder — new payment account
Please transfer 280 today to our replacement account.
Do not call the usual number: our phone system is down.
Open the attached account-update.exe to see the details.
Reply with your login verification code so we can confirm you.
```

The existing invoice in Taylor's private records has different payment details. An independently verified supplier contact is available. A voice message later sounds like the regular contact but its origin is unverified.

## Your task

Write the sequence you would follow before any payment, download, or reply. Draft an independent verification question. Explain which information you would keep private, and demonstrate a harmless sample backup restore separately.

Produce a short action plan, relevant calculations, and any message requested. For an independent attempt, close the guide and do not open the explanation. Record any help used; practical actions need separate evidence.

## Advanced variation

Taylor already opened the attachment or disclosed a code. Outline prompt official provider/device-support and relevant bank/reporting contacts; do not claim a generic password change fixes every incident.

## Assessment criteria

| Stage | Demonstrate | Competency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foundation | Explain why email, unique passwords, updates, and MFA matter. | `digital-safety.foundation` |
| Applied | Use official settings to review account protection and restore a sample backup. | `digital-safety.applied` |
| Independent | Handle a suspicious message without clicking its link or sharing codes. | `digital-safety.independent` |
| Advanced Scenario | Adapt after a possible account compromise or realistic impersonation. | `digital-safety.advanced-scenario` |

Apply the [assessment rules](../curriculum/mastery.md): correct reasoning, feasible action, verification, appropriate boundaries, and recorded help. Do not award a practical competency from a written plan alone.

## Suggested solution

<details>
<summary>Open after your attempt</summary>

Hold the payment, do not run the unexpected executable, and do not share authentication codes. Contact the supplier through an independently verified channel from existing records, not details in the new message. Ask whether the invoice and account change are genuine. A familiar-sounding recording does not establish provenance. For a possible compromise, use the relevant official account/device recovery and bank/fraud routes promptly according to the incident. Record the sample backup restore separately: describing it is not performing it. No contact details, codes, or account numbers belong in repository evidence.

Other solutions can meet the criteria when they are correct, feasible, and safely scoped. Record what you would change before a fresh delayed attempt.

</details>
