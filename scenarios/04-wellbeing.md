# S04 · Too many problems at once

[Scenario library](README.md) · [Guide](../guides/04-wellbeing.md) · [Competencies](../domains/wellbeing.md)

**Time:** about 20 minutes; split practical tasks across sessions. All people, documents, amounts, and events are fictional. Use local official guidance for real situations.

## Context

Casey has slept poorly, feels overwhelmed, and is avoiding a school/work task. They want a plan for today, not a diagnosis.

## Materials

| Material | Information |
| --- | --- |
| Task | Prepare a short presentation for tomorrow; topic chosen, outline not started |
| Basic needs | Lunch not yet eaten; an evening rest period is possible |
| Contacts | Friend can listen; tutor/supervisor can clarify the task; verified professional support route not yet recorded |
| Belief | Casey thinks asking for help means failing |

## Your task

Separate the problem into manageable parts. Choose a first action, protect a basic need, and draft a specific support request. Identify a professional-support route for persistent or worsening difficulties without assigning a diagnosis.

Produce a short action plan, relevant calculations, and any message requested. For an independent attempt, close the guide and do not open the explanation. Record any help used; practical actions need separate evidence.

## Advanced variation

The friend is unavailable and the distress is worsening. Explain the fallback support plan and when urgent support takes priority over finishing the task.

## Assessment criteria

| Stage | Demonstrate | Competency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foundation | Identify overload signs and distinguish a manageable task from a need for more support. | `wellbeing.foundation` |
| Applied | Use a prompt to build a private support plan and divide a problem into steps. | `wellbeing.applied` |
| Independent | Respond to the overloaded-week scenario with a feasible plan and a clear support request. | `wellbeing.independent` |
| Advanced Scenario | Adapt when the first contact is unavailable and identify when urgent professional help takes priority. | `wellbeing.advanced-scenario` |

Apply the [assessment rules](../curriculum/mastery.md): correct reasoning, feasible action, verification, appropriate boundaries, and recorded help. Do not award a practical competency from a written plan alone.

## Suggested solution

<details>
<summary>Open after your attempt</summary>

A possible plan is to eat, write three outline points, and ask the tutor/supervisor which part to prioritise. Request something specific: a short clarification or conversation. A friend may listen but does not replace professional care. Verify and record an appropriate local care route; use urgent support when needed and emergency services for immediate danger. If the friend cannot answer, use another trusted contact or the relevant verified professional route. Do not grade Casey's feelings or require task completion as proof of wellbeing.

Other solutions can meet the criteria when they are correct, feasible, and safely scoped. Record what you would change before a fresh delayed attempt.

</details>
