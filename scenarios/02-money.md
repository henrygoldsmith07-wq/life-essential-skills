# S02 · The month that balances but runs short

[Scenario library](README.md) · [Guide](../guides/02-money.md) · [Competencies](../domains/money.md)

**Time:** about 20 minutes; split practical tasks across sessions. All people, documents, amounts, and events are fictional. Use local official guidance for real situations.

## Context

Alex has 1,000 available at the start of the month and is paid on day 28. Amounts are fictional pounds; no overdraft, other savings, or credit is assumed.

## Materials

| Statement or planned payment | Day | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rent | 1 | -650 |
| Utilities bill | 3 | -100 |
| Transport | 5 | -80 |
| Phone bill | 7 | -25 |
| Debt payment | 10 | -60 |
| Food | 15 | -120 |
| Net pay | 28 | +1,600 |
| Remaining food | 30 | -140 |
| Flexible spending | 30 | -120 |
| Savings transfer | 30 | -50 |
| Irregular-cost reserve transfer | 30 | -90 |

A repair costing 220 becomes due on day 16. Offer A lends 220: a 10 upfront fee plus four monthly repayments of 60, beginning next month on day 28. Offer B lends 220: five repayments of 52, first due on day 20. No other fees are stated; ask about omitted terms.

## Your task

Calculate planned monthly outgoings, unallocated income, and the lowest dated balance before pay. Add the repair and explain both the monthly and timing problems. Compare the total repayment and timing of the offers. Propose questions and legitimate support routes instead of assuming borrowing is affordable.

Produce a short action plan, relevant calculations, and any message requested. For an independent attempt, close the guide and do not open the explanation. Record any help used; practical actions need separate evidence.

## Advanced variation

Pay is delayed by three days. Explain what must be checked or negotiated early; future reductions in flexible spending do not automatically solve today's shortfall.

## Assessment criteria

## Suggested solution

<details>
<summary>Open after your attempt</summary>

Outgoings/allocations are 1,435; income minus allocations is 165. The running balance falls to -35 on day 15, before the repair. With the 220 repair it falls to -255 on day 16; monthly allocations become 1,655, a 55 gap. Original closing available balance is 1,165, not 165, because it includes the opening 1,000. A totals 250 (cost 30); B totals 260 (cost 40). B also requires payment before payday; A's immediate fee and future payments still need affordability checks. Removing future savings/flexible allocations may help the month but does not create cash before payday. Check the bill/repair urgency and any legitimate payment arrangements or advice, protect essential needs, and verify complete credit terms. Neither offer is automatically the right choice.

Other solutions can meet the criteria when they are correct, feasible, and safely scoped. Record what you would change before a fresh delayed attempt.

</details>
