# 05 · Communication, relationships, and boundaries

[Curriculum](../README.md) · [Next: Critical thinking](06-thinking.md)

**Goal:** express a need clearly and listen without assuming you know the other person's meaning.

## Learn

A useful conversation separates observations from interpretations. "You interrupted me twice" describes behaviour; "You never care" assumes a motive. Explain the behaviour, its effect, and your request. Then leave room for a reply.

Listen by checking your understanding: "It sounds like you need more notice; have I understood?" You can understand someone's feelings without agreeing to every request. A boundary describes what you will accept and what you will do, rather than attempting to control another person.

Consent means freely agreeing to a particular interaction. Ask rather than assume, respect a refusal, and understand that someone can change their mind. Pressure is not agreement. Respect privacy when discussing someone else's life.

When a disagreement escalates, agree a pause and a time to return if doing so is safe. Threats, coercion, or violence require attention to safety and appropriate local support. You do not have to negotiate a boundary with someone who is endangering you.

## Example

"When plans change at the last minute, I struggle to arrange transport. Could you let me know by lunchtime? If I do not hear by then, I will make my own plans."

## Practise

1. Rewrite an accusation as an observation, effect, and request.
2. Practise summarising a friend's point and asking if you understood.
3. Write one boundary with an action you can actually take.
4. After a conversation, note one thing you understood better.

## Self-check

1. How does a request differ from a demand?
2. What makes a boundary workable?
3. Does a previous agreement guarantee consent now?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answers</summary>

1. A request allows a genuine response, including refusal or discussion.
2. It is clear, specific, and includes an action within your control.
3. No. Check the current situation and respect a changed decision.

</details>

**Done when:** you can make a specific request, accurately summarise a reply, and describe one boundary.

**Teaching note:** the scripts are practice tools, not guarantees that a conversation will succeed. Adapt them to your culture, access needs, and safety.
