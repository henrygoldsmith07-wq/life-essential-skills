# 02 · Money, borrowing, and a financial buffer

[Curriculum](../README.md) · [Next: Physical health](03-health.md)

**Goal:** understand where your money goes and make a realistic plan for the next month.

## Learn

A budget compares money coming in with money going out. Use take-home income, essentials, other spending, debt payments, savings, and irregular costs. Also track dates: a positive monthly balance does not guarantee you can pay a bill before payday.

An emergency fund is a reserve for unexpected costs. Its size depends on your circumstances; begin with a manageable target based on a plausible expense. Keep it accessible and understand any account restrictions. If essential bills exceed income, a stricter spreadsheet will not fix the gap on its own: look for legitimate local support or qualified debt advice.

Before borrowing, compare total repayment, interest, fees, repayment dates, penalties, and consequences of missing payments. A small monthly payment can conceal a long and expensive agreement. Investing involves risk; learn about fees, diversification, access, and potential losses before making personal decisions. Tax and pension rules vary by country.

## Example

Fictional monthly amounts in any currency: income 1,500; essentials 1,000; other spending 200; debt payments 100; irregular-cost allowance 100. That leaves 100 for savings or another planned purpose. If income arrives after rent is due, you also need a cash-flow plan.

## Practise

1. Complete a local copy of the [budget](../templates/budget.md) using estimates or fictional numbers.
2. Check your arithmetic and list the dates of major bills.
3. Choose one realistic adjustment and a small buffer goal.
4. Read a sample credit agreement and identify total repayment and fees.

## Self-check

1. How is cash flow different from a monthly budget?
2. Why compare total repayment rather than only monthly payments?
3. What should you do if essentials exceed income?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answers</summary>

1. Cash flow includes the timing of incoming and outgoing money.
2. Total repayment reveals the overall cost, including the effect of duration and fees.
3. Identify the gap and seek legitimate support or qualified advice; do not assume unrealistic cuts solve it.

</details>

**Done when:** your budget balances arithmetically and you can explain your next bill date, spending adjustment, and buffer goal.
