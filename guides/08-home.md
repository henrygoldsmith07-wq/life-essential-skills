# 08 · Cooking, cleaning, and everyday independence

[Curriculum](../README.md) · [Next: Work and earning](09-work.md)

**Goal:** prepare one affordable meal and organise a manageable household routine.

## Learn

Start with meals you can make using the equipment, food, and time available to you. Plan a few meals before shopping, check what you already have, compare unit prices, and make a short list. A simple starting combination is a starch, a protein source, and vegetables, adapted to your needs and allergies.

Food safety includes cleaning, avoiding cross-contamination, cooking properly, and safe chilling. Keep raw ingredients that may contaminate ready-to-eat food separate. Follow package instructions and official guidance for cooking and storage; appearance alone is not a universal test of safety.

Read cleaning-product labels and do not mix products. Use appliances according to their instructions. For household maintenance, first identify who is responsible and when qualified help is required. Learn where your utility controls are from a responsible person or professional; do not experiment with gas or electrical systems.

## Example

For a simple meal, choose tinned beans, bread or a grain, and suitable vegetables. Follow the food's preparation instructions. Check allergens, clean your preparation area, and consult storage guidance if saving leftovers. Record the cost so you can compare it with another meal.

## Practise

1. Plan and prepare one meal appropriate to your abilities and equipment.
2. Read the relevant cooking and storage instructions before starting.
3. Write a short weekly routine for dishes, laundry, cleaning, and rubbish.
4. Identify the person or service to contact for a repair.

## Self-check

1. What are the main areas of food-safety practice?
2. Why read cleaning-product labels before use?
3. When should a repair be passed to qualified help?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answers</summary>

1. Cleaning, preventing cross-contamination, cooking, and chilling/storage.
2. To follow safe use, ventilation, and other instructions and avoid incompatible products.
3. When it involves hazardous systems, exceeds your competence, or requires a qualified person.

</details>

**Done when:** you have made one meal safely and created a household routine you can follow.
