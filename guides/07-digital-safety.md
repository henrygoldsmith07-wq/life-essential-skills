# 07 · Digital safety and scam awareness

[Curriculum](../README.md) · [Next: Everyday independence](08-home.md)

**Goal:** protect your main email, verify suspicious requests, and recover an important file.

## Learn

Your email is often the route to resetting other accounts. Give it a strong, unique password and enable two-step verification where available. A password manager helps create and store distinct passwords. Keep software updated and back up important files. Store account recovery information securely outside this repository.

Scams often use urgency, secrecy, impersonation, or an unexpected payment request. Verify through a channel you found independently: open the organisation's official app or contact it using a known number. Do not use a suspicious message's link or number to verify that same message. Never give an unexpected caller your password or authentication codes.

If you suspect an account or payment is compromised, use the provider's official recovery or fraud process promptly. The right next step depends on the incident; independently contact your bank or account provider as relevant.

## Example

A message claims your bank account will close unless you confirm details immediately. You open the bank's official app yourself or use its independently verified contact details. The message's urgency does not establish that it is genuine.

## Practise

1. Review the security settings of your main email.
2. Set a unique password and enable two-step verification if offered.
3. Check the provider's recovery options without writing secrets here.
4. Back up a harmless sample file, then restore it to confirm the backup works.
5. Explain how you would verify a surprise payment request.

## Self-check

1. Why prioritise your main email?
2. Does a familiar logo prove a message is authentic?
3. How do you know a backup is usable?
