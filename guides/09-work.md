# 09 · Work, earning, and showing your skills

[Curriculum](../README.md) · [Next: Learning and time](10-learning-and-time.md)

**Goal:** show evidence of a useful skill and evaluate an opportunity before committing.

## Learn

Employers and clients need to understand what you can do. Describe a concrete task, what you did, and what happened. Evidence can come from study, volunteering, caring responsibilities, personal projects, or paid work. You do not need to invent experience to write a useful CV.

Read an opportunity closely: responsibilities, location, hours, pay, training, eligibility, and selection process. Research the organisation independently. Be cautious about requests for upfront payments or sensitive identity information before you have verified the opportunity.

Prepare for interviews by matching examples to the role's requirements. Use a simple structure: situation, task, action, result, and what you learned. Ask questions about expectations and working conditions. Before accepting an offer, read the written terms and seek qualified local advice about anything unclear. Employment rules depend on where you live.

## Example

"Organised a four-person study group, scheduled weekly meetings, and created shared revision questions. Everyone completed the planned topics before the exam." This is more informative than "excellent leadership skills" and does not require an invented statistic.

## Practise

1. Choose a real or sample opportunity and list its three main requirements.
2. Write one evidence-based example for each requirement.
3. Practise a two-minute interview answer aloud.
4. Draft three questions about the role, supervision, and written terms.
5. Identify one skill gap and a small project that could demonstrate progress.

## Self-check

1. What makes a CV statement credible?
2. How can you prepare when you have little paid experience?
3. What should you verify before sharing sensitive documents?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answers</summary>

1. A specific truthful action and outcome you can explain.
2. Use relevant evidence from learning, projects, volunteering, or responsibilities.
3. The organisation, opportunity, recipient, purpose, and secure process.

</details>

**Done when:** you have three truthful examples and can explain one in an interview format.
