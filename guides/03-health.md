# 03 · Physical health and everyday routines

[Curriculum](../README.md) · [Next: Emotional wellbeing](04-wellbeing.md)

**Goal:** build one sustainable routine and know how to access healthcare.

## Learn

Start with small routines you can repeat. Plan varied food, regular movement suited to your abilities, time for sleep, and basic hygiene. WHO guidance supports a varied diet built around foods such as vegetables, fruit, wholegrains, pulses, and appropriate protein sources. Regular physical activity supports physical and mental health. Specific needs and activity recommendations vary with age, health, disability, and pregnancy.

Make a habit concrete: say what you will do, when, and what the smallest version looks like. For example, choose a comfortable walk or accessible movement break after lunch. A smaller plan that happens is useful progress.

Learn how routine and urgent healthcare work in your area. Keep track of appointments privately and bring questions to a clinician. Use local guidance for vaccinations and screening. Ask a clinician or pharmacist about medications; do not change prescribed treatment based on a learning exercise.

## Example

You want more consistent evenings. Your experiment is to prepare tomorrow's breakfast and begin winding down at a similar time on three evenings. Record whether the routine was practical; change the setup if it was not.

## Practise

1. Choose one food, movement, hygiene, or sleep routine.
2. Define a two-minute version for difficult days.
3. Try it for three days and record what helped.
4. Find your local route to routine care and urgent advice.

## Self-check

1. Why should a routine fit your actual circumstances?
2. How can you make an oversized goal easier to begin?
3. Who can answer questions about prescribed medicines?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answers</summary>

1. A practical routine accounts for your health, ability, resources, culture, and schedule.
2. Choose a small action, a clear cue, and a realistic frequency.
3. A qualified clinician or pharmacist familiar with the relevant treatment.

</details>

**Done when:** you have tried one routine and know how to contact an appropriate healthcare service.
