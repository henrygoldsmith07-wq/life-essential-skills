# 10 · Learning, priorities, and using your time

[Curriculum](../README.md) · [Next: Life administration](11-life-admin.md)

**Goal:** turn a vague intention into a scheduled action and check whether you learned it.

## Learn

Define a skill as something observable: "I can explain my budget" is easier to assess than "I understand money." After reading, close the material and retrieve the main ideas from memory. Check and correct mistakes. Return to the skill on later days rather than relying only on one long session. Research on retrieval and spacing supports these approaches to learning.

For planning, collect tasks in one place, identify real deadlines, and choose a small number of priorities. Estimate time and leave room for interruptions. Separate fixed commitments from flexible work. A calendar full of imaginary capacity makes it harder to adapt.

Attach a new action to a reliable cue: "After breakfast, I will review one guide for ten minutes." If that is too large, reduce it. Your weekly review should ask what happened and what to change, rather than only whether you followed the original plan.

## Example

Instead of "get organised," schedule ten minutes on Sunday to check deadlines, pick three priorities, and prepare for the first one. For a learning task, write three questions and answer them again later without looking.

## Practise

1. Run the 20-minute cycle in [START-HERE](../START-HERE.md).
2. Write three recall questions about the guide you studied.
3. Schedule two short reviews on different days.
4. Complete a [weekly review](../templates/weekly-review.md) and choose a smaller next action if needed.

## Self-check

1. How does retrieval differ from rereading?
2. Why return to a skill over several days?
3. What can you change if the plan repeatedly fails?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answers</summary>

1. Retrieval requires producing an answer from memory before checking it.
2. Spacing practice supports longer-term retention and reveals forgotten material.
3. Reduce the action, change the cue, remove a barrier, or revise unrealistic capacity estimates.

</details>

**Done when:** you have completed a learning cycle and scheduled a later recall session.
