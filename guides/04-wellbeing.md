# 04 · Emotional wellbeing and asking for help

[Curriculum](../README.md) · [Next: Relationships](05-relationships.md)

**Goal:** recognise when you are struggling and choose a manageable next step or source of support.

## Learn

Stress can affect concentration, sleep, mood, and daily routines. Notice your own patterns without turning every difficult feeling into a diagnosis. You can write down what happened, how you felt, and what you need next.

For an everyday problem, separate what you can act on from what you cannot control today. Choose one small action, make time for a restorative activity, and connect with someone you trust. Self-help can support wellbeing, but persistent or worsening difficulties deserve professional attention.

Build a support plan before you need it: trusted people, your route to professional care, and local urgent help. If you or someone else is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services. For urgent mental-health support, use a verified service in your country.

## Example

An unfinished assignment feels overwhelming. Write the specific problem: "I do not know how to begin the introduction." Your next step is to outline three points and ask for feedback. If the distress keeps disrupting your daily life, add a conversation with an appropriate support professional.

## Practise

1. Identify two early signs that you are overloaded.
2. List two trusted contacts and verify a local professional support route. Store details privately.
3. Pick a low-pressure activity that helps you reset.
4. Practise this request: "I have been finding [situation] difficult. Could you help me with [specific action]?"

## Self-check

1. What is one action within your control today?
2. When is more support appropriate?
3. What should a support plan include?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answers</summary>

1. A specific manageable step, such as arranging a conversation or starting a small task.
2. When distress persists, worsens, disrupts daily life, or creates a safety concern.
3. Trusted people, professional care options, and verified local urgent support.

</details>

**Done when:** you can name a small coping action and an appropriate person or service to contact.
