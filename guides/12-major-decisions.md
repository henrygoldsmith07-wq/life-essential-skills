# 12 · Major decisions, transport, and your community

[Curriculum](../README.md) · [Start the 30-day plan](../learning-plan/30-days.md)

**Goal:** compare a major life choice using costs, practical needs, risks, and reliable local information.

## Learn

Housing, education, transport, and care arrangements affect several parts of life at once. Compare the full cost, your daily needs, access requirements, time commitment, and what happens if circumstances change. Separate what you must have from what would merely be nice.

For housing, investigate total recurring and upfront costs, location, condition, transport access, responsibilities, and written terms. Use current local official guidance for your rights. For travel, plan the route, cost, accessibility, last service, and an alternative. Follow local road rules and get appropriate instruction for driving or cycling.

Before committing to a course, examine entry requirements, accreditation where relevant, total cost, completion expectations, and evidence about outcomes. Consider whether a smaller experiment could test your interest first.

Community knowledge also supports independence. Learn how to contact your local authority, access libraries and community services, check local alerts, and find official information about civic participation. Think about how your choices affect shared spaces, resources, and other people.

## Example

Home A has rent of 700 and travel costs of 150 per month. Home B has rent of 800 and travel costs of 40. A costs 850 and B costs 840 before other expenses. The lower rent does not necessarily mean a lower total cost; compare commute time and other needs too.

## Practise

1. Choose a real or fictional decision about housing, transport, or education.
2. List your three most important needs and two realistic options.
3. Compare full costs, time, access, risks, and exit conditions in the [decision journal](../templates/decision-journal.md).
4. Identify missing facts and verify one through an appropriate official source.
5. Find one useful local community service.

## Self-check

1. Why is the headline price insufficient?
2. How can you reduce uncertainty before committing?
3. What should a transport backup plan include?

<details>
<summary>Suggested answers</summary>

1. Other costs, time, constraints, and responsibilities may change which option works best.
2. Verify missing facts, get appropriate advice, or run a smaller reversible experiment.
3. A feasible alternative route or service, its cost and availability, and a way to contact help if needed.

</details>

**Done when:** you can explain your preferred option, its main tradeoff, and one fact you still need to check.
