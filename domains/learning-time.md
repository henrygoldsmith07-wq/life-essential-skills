# 10 · Learning and time: skills and mastery

[Short guide](../guides/10-learning-and-time.md) · [Scenario S10](../scenarios/10-learning-time.md) · [Map](../curriculum/dependency-map.md)

## Small skills to practise

Choose one row at a time. The guide introduces the domain; these actions deepen it without requiring a long lesson.

| Subskill | Learn or practise | Demonstrate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Priorities | Separate real deadlines, consequences, and preferences. | Choose three priorities with reasons. |
| Calendar | Include travel and preparation in commitments. | Detect a scheduling clash. |
| Task breakdown | Choose a small observable next action. | Split a vague task into manageable steps. |
| Retrieval | Answer from memory before checking. | Produce and correct a closed-guide response. |
| Spacing | Revisit skills on later days. | Complete a delayed fresh demonstration. |
| Interruptions | Leave buffer and plan a fallback. | Adapt a calendar after a disruption. |
| Weekly review | Compare plans with actual practice. | Change one unrealistic assumption. |
| Capacity | Count available time and basic needs. | Explain what must be deferred or delegated. |

## Observable mastery

| Stage | Competency ID | Criterion | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Foundation | `learning-time.foundation` | Identify fixed commitments and explain the difference between retrieval and rereading. | A commitment list and memory-first question. |
| Applied | `learning-time.applied` | Use a checklist to schedule realistic tasks, travel, and two later reviews. | A calendar with buffer. |
| Independent | `learning-time.independent` | Resolve a busy-week conflict by comparing consequences and contacting affected people. | An independent schedule and communication plan. |
| Advanced Scenario | `learning-time.advanced-scenario` | Adapt to a new interruption and use delayed reassessment to revise the plan. | A feasible replacement plan and recorded reassessment. |

Use the [assessment rules](../curriculum/mastery.md) before awarding a stage. A written scenario demonstrates judgement and planning, not certification or physical competence. Record practical demonstrations separately where a criterion requires them.
